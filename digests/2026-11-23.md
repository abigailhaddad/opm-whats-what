## 2. OPM sets fixed deadlines for skills-based rewrite of job standards

For each of 22 cycles of new standards, agencies must apply them to new and reclassified positions within 120 days and review every existing position in the covered series within 365 days, with no extension for prioritization. Agencies that use optional Super Intelligence tools must name a human reviewer of record and keep prompts and outputs in the classification record.

*[Modernizing Federal Classification and Qualification Standards Around Skills (Reclassification and Requalification Initiative)](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/modernizing-federal-classification-and-qualification-standards-around-skills.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-16) · [Issuance of the Super Intelligence-Assisted Position Review Toolkit](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/issuance-of-the-si-assisted-position-review-toolkit.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-16)*

- **OPM sets a fixed 365-day deadline for agencies to review every existing position in each cycle's series, and says prioritization does not extend it**. For each of the 22 cycles, agencies must finish reviewing existing positions in the covered series within 365 days of issuance. OPM states that ordering the work by mission-critical roles, incumbent counts or hiring volume does not extend the completion date, and agencies must report completion and material findings to OPM when each review period closes. (p. 3)
- **OPM requires agencies to apply each new standard to new and reclassified positions within 120 days, when it replaces the old standard**. Within 120 days of each cycle's issuance, agencies must use the updated standards for new and reclassified positions in the covered series. On that date each new standard supersedes the prior classification standard and qualification requirements for the series. (p. 3)
- **OPM tells agencies to document the human reviewer and keep SI prompts and outputs in the classification record**. For any classification determination informed by SI output, agencies must name a human reviewer of record and retain the prompts and outputs as part of the classification record. The memo also bars submitting personally identifiable information or non-public data to any tool not approved under the agency's SI governance, privacy, and records policies. (p. 3)

## 3. OPM finalizes FERS survivor factors that cut early-start annuities

Revised factors, which OPM says the law required after the Board of Actuaries changed its demographic assumptions, produce lower benefits for spouses or former spouses of deceased separated employees who elect an earlier survivor annuity. OPM rejected three commenters' objections without changing the rule.

*[Federal Employees' Retirement System; Present Value Conversion Factors for Spouses of Deceased Separated Employees](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/20/2026-23729/federal-employees-retirement-system-present-value-conversion-factors-for-spouses-of-deceased) (Rule, 2026-11-20)*

- **OPM acknowledges the new factors reduce survivor annuities for spouses who elect early payment**. OPM agreed with one commenter that, compared with the previous factors, the updated factors produce lower benefit amounts for spouses or former spouses who elect an earlier survivor annuity. The summary describes only a revision of the table and does not say benefits go down. (p. 75171)
- **OPM says the law gives it no discretion to keep the old factors once the Board of Actuaries changed its assumptions**. OPM rejected a commenter's claim that the rule was inherently controversial, citing 5 U.S.C. 8461(i), which requires corresponding factor changes when actuarial assumptions change. OPM said the Board kept its economic assumptions (including 4.00 percent interest) but changed demographic assumptions, including mortality improvement, at its 2025 meeting. (p. 75171)
- **OPM rejected the 3 commenters' objections to the factors without changing the rule**. Of 7 comments, 3 raised concerns about the revised factors; 2 said OPM gave too little information on actuarial assumptions. OPM said none identified an error in the assumptions or calculation, and none caused it to change the rule; 2 comments supported the rule and 2 were unrelated. (p. 75170)

## 4. OPM keeps eFile mandate for claims despite commenters' tolling requests

OPM refused commenters' requests to automatically toll filing deadlines while eFile exemption requests are pending, for both FLSA claims and other administrative claims, leaving relief case-by-case. For administrative claims, OPM said documented eFile outages and disability or literacy barriers will weigh toward good cause for late filings.

*[Procedures for Settling Claims](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/20/2026-23731/procedures-for-settling-claims) (Rule, 2026-11-20) · [FLSA Claims and Compliance](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/20/2026-23730/flsa-claims-and-compliance) (Rule, 2026-11-20)*

- **OPM refused to set a response deadline or tolling period for eFile exemption requests and outages, keeping exemptions in its 'sole and exclusive discretion'**. OPM declined requests for a fixed outage tolling period (Comment 06), a fixed response deadline for exemption requests (Comments 03 and 05), and tolling of the filing deadline when an exemption is requested (Comment 02). A claimant who fails to ask for an exemption before the filing deadline still will not get a good-cause finding for lateness, so the rule's timing risk stays with claimants. (p. 75165)
- **OPM refused to adopt automatic tolling of filing deadlines while an eFile exemption request is pending**. Comment 03 proposed regulatory text that would toll a deadline during an exemption request and treat a paper filing as filed on its original date once an exemption issues. OPM declined, saying this would change how Sec. 551.702(c) sets the date the back-pay claim period is preserved. Relief stays case-by-case under existing reconsideration and 45-day waiver provisions. (p. 75167)
- **OPM said eFile outages, disability, or literacy barriers will count toward good cause for late filings or late exemption requests**. OPM stated it will weigh a documented agency-side eFile outage and a claimant's literacy, disability, or other barrier to using eFile when deciding good cause. It also said a timely good-faith attempt to contact OPM by phone, email, or non-electronic filing bears directly on good cause. These are statements of practice, not changes to the regulatory text. (p. 75165)
