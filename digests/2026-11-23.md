## 1. OPM starts rewriting 600-plus job standards around skills, beginning with eight series

OPM will rewrite classification and qualification standards for more than 600 occupational series in 22 cycles of about 30 series each. The first cycle covers eight administrative series and about 220,000 positions, but the memo sets March 22, 2027 for applying the standards to new positions while its FAQ's 120-day rule points to March 16, 2027.

*[Modernizing Federal Classification and Qualification Standards Around Skills (Reclassification and Requalification Initiative)](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/modernizing-federal-classification-and-qualification-standards-around-skills.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-16) · [Issuance of Competency Based Classification Standards for Administrative Occupational Series](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/issuance-of-competency-based-classification-standards-for-administrative-occupational-series.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-16)*

- **OPM will rewrite classification and qualification standards for 600+ series in 22 cycles of about 30 series each**. Standards will be issued on a continuing cycle, each release including the classification standard, qualification requirements, and crosswalks from old series definitions and titles. OPM can adjust the roughly 30-series cycle size based on evidence review and agency feedback. (p. 2)
- **OPM says the new standards touch about 220,000 positions and tells agencies to inventory every filled and vacant position in the eight series, plus positions in adjacent series**. Agencies must list all encumbered and vacant positions in the covered series. They must also list positions in other series whose duties may fall within the revised occupational definitions, which widens the review beyond the eight series. (p. 2)
- **The FAQ gives the new-position deadline as 120 days after issuance, which is March 16, 2027, six days earlier than the memo's March 22, 2027**. The memo body states a fixed date of March 22, 2027 for applying the standards to new and reclassified positions. FAQ 4 instead says 'no later than 120 days after the date of this issuance,' which counts to March 16, 2027 from the November 16, 2026 memo date. The memo does not say which controls. (p. 4)

OPM confirmed two direct final rules requiring claims to be filed through eFile. Four of five commenters on the claims-settlement rule asked OPM to withdraw it, and OPM refused to keep permanent paper and email filing for survivors, retirees and pro se claimants. ## 3. OPM requires a human reviewer of record for AI-assisted position reviews

Agencies that use Super Intelligence tools to review position descriptions must name a human reviewer of record and keep prompts and outputs in the classification record. They may not put personally identifiable information or non-public data into unapproved tools. OPM can expand the toolkit without issuing a new memo.

*[Issuance of the Super Intelligence-Assisted Position Review Toolkit](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/issuance-of-the-si-assisted-position-review-toolkit.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-16)*

- **OPM requires agencies to name a human reviewer of record and keep SI prompts and outputs in the classification record**. For every determination informed by SI output, agencies must document the human reviewer and keep the prompts and outputs as part of the classification record. The memo's summary text does not mention this recordkeeping duty. (p. 3)
- **OPM bars agencies from putting personally identifiable information or non-public data into SI tools not approved under agency governance policies**. Agencies may not submit personally identifiable information or non-public data to any SI tool that lacks approval under the agency's SI governance, privacy, and records policies. The restriction applies to the toolkit's prompts and to any other SI-assisted review done under the standards. (p. 3)
- **OPM says it can expand the toolkit without reissuing any memorandum**. OPM will add to the toolkit based on agency experience, and such revisions will not require a new memo. Agencies will have to follow the toolkit as it is updated rather than as it stood on the issuance date. (p. 1)
