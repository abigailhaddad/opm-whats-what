OPM will replace classification and qualification standards for more than 600 occupational series in 22 cycles starting in FY2027. The first eight administrative series take effect March 22, 2027, with all existing positions to be reviewed by November 29, 2027, though OPM's memo and FAQ give conflicting start dates.

- **OPM will replace standards for more than 600 occupational series in 22 cycles of about 30 series each, starting in FY2027**. - **OPM requires agencies to review all existing positions in the eight series by November 29, 2027, and says mission priorities cannot extend that date**. The new standards replace the old classification standards on March 22, 2027, and agencies must apply them to new and reclassified positions by then. Agencies must finish reviewing existing positions by November 29, 2027, and can only sequence the work within that window. (p. 2)
- **OPM's memo and its FAQ give different start dates and describe the review window inconsistently**. The memo says March 22, 2027, but the FAQ says new and reclassified positions must be covered 120 days after the issuance date, which falls on March 16, 2027. ## 2. OPM keeps lower FERS survivor annuity factors after three commenters object

OPM confirmed revised FERS early-commencement factors that it acknowledges cut monthly survivor annuities for spouses who elect earlier payment. It said federal law gave it no discretion to keep the old factors and found none of the 7 comments, including 3 raising concerns, significant enough to withdraw the rule.

*[Federal Employees' Retirement System; Present Value Conversion Factors for Spouses of Deceased Separated Employees](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/20/2026-23729/federal-employees-retirement-system-present-value-conversion-factors-for-spouses-of-deceased) (Rule, 2026-11-20)*

- **OPM acknowledges the new factors cut monthly survivor annuities for spouses who elect earlier payment**. OPM agreed with one commenter that the updated factors produce lower benefit amounts than the previous factors for survivors who elect an earlier commencing date. The notice's summary describes only a revision of the table and does not say benefits go down. (p. 75171)
- **OPM says federal law leaves it no discretion to keep the old factors once the Board of Actuaries changed its assumptions**. OPM rejected a commenter's claim that the rule was inherently controversial, citing 5 U.S.C. 8461(i), which requires corresponding factor changes when actuarial assumptions change. OPM said the commenter identified no error in the factors or assumptions and no legal alternative preserving the old factors. (p. 75171)
- **OPM treated 7 comments, including 3 raising concerns about the revision, as not significant adverse, so the direct final rule was not withdrawn**. Of 7 timely comments, 2 supported the rule, 2 were unrelated, and 3 raised concerns. OPM judged none of them required reconsideration, new analysis, or a substantive change, and confirmed the August 24, 2026 effective date. (p. 75170)

## 3. OPM keeps eFile mandate for claims, rejecting commenters' tolling and paper-filing requests

OPM refused to toll FLSA claim deadlines while a claimant's eFile exemption request is pending, and rejected four of five commenters' requests to withdraw the claims-settlement eFile rule and keep permanent paper and email filing for survivors, retirees and others. OPM did clarify that exemption requests can be made by phone or email, not just through its website.

*[FLSA Claims and Compliance](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/20/2026-23730/flsa-claims-and-compliance) (Rule, 2026-11-20) · [Procedures for Settling Claims](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/20/2026-23731/procedures-for-settling-claims) (Rule, 2026-11-20)*

- **OPM refused to toll deadlines while a claimant's eFile exemption request is pending**. Comments 03, 05, and 06 warned that a claimant who cannot file electronically could lose back pay while waiting on an exemption, since recovery runs from the date OPM or the agency receives the claim. OPM declined to adopt Comment 03's automatic tolling or backdating text, saying it would change how the claim date is set, and instead said it will weigh timely good-faith contact case by case under existing reconsideration and waiver provisions. (p. 75167)
- **OPM rejected requests from four of five commenters to withdraw the rule and keep permanent paper and email filing for survivors, retirees and others**. Comments 03, 04, 05 and 06 asked OPM to withdraw the direct final rule and use notice-and-comment rulemaking; Comment 06 proposed keeping mail and email intake permanently for survivors, veterans' beneficiaries, retirees and pro se claimants. OPM said the exemption process and the agency-assisted filing option already provide an accommodation, so permanent parallel intake is not necessary. (p. 75165)
- **OPM will accept exemption requests from eFiling by phone or email, not just through its website**. After commenters said an online-only exemption path trapped offline filers, OPM said a party can request an exemption using the phone number or email address in the rule's contact caption, and that a timely phone or written request will be treated the same as a website request. This is a clarification in the preamble to the exemption provision, not a change to the regulatory text. (p. 75165)

Four of five commenters (02, 03, 05, 06) argued that the direct final rule procedure was improper.
