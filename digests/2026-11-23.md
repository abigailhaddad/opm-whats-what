## 1. OPM begins rewriting federal job standards, starting with 220,000 administrative positions

OPM will rewrite classification and qualification standards for more than 600 occupational series in 22 cycles starting in FY 2027. The first batch covers eight administrative series (about 220,000 positions): new standards replace the old ones March 22, 2027, and agencies must finish reviewing every existing position in those series by November 29, 2027.

*[Modernizing Federal Classification and Qualification Standards Around Skills (Reclassification and Requalification Initiative)](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/modernizing-federal-classification-and-qualification-standards-around-skills.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-16) · [Issuance of Competency Based Classification Standards for Administrative Occupational Series](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/issuance-of-competency-based-classification-standards-for-administrative-occupational-series.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-16) · [Issuance of the Super Intelligence-Assisted Position Review Toolkit](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/issuance-of-the-si-assisted-position-review-toolkit.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-16)*

- **OPM sets March 22, 2027 as the date the new standards replace the old ones for eight administrative series covering about 220,000 positions**. The new standards for series 0301, 0306, 0308, 0340, 0341, 0343, 0346 and 2291 supersede the prior position classification standards on that date. OPM says these series encompass approximately 220,000 positions governmentwide. (p. 2)
- **OPM will roll out the standards in 22 cycles of about 30 series each, starting in FY 2027, with the cycle size open to adjustment**. Agencies should plan for 22 issuance cycles covering over 600 series, with about 30 series per cycle. OPM says it may adjust that number based on evidence review and agency feedback, and will identify series in scope before each publication. (p. 2)
- **OPM gives agencies until November 29, 2027 to finish reviewing every existing position in the covered series and says mission priorities do not extend that date**. Agencies must apply the standards to new and reclassified positions by March 22, 2027 and complete the review of existing positions by November 29, 2027. Agency prioritization of mission-critical work may set the order of review but does not extend the completion date. The FAQ says agencies with constraints should contact OPM before a deadline passes. (p. 2)

## 2. OPM confirms new FERS survivor factors that lower early-annuity benefits

OPM acknowledged that its updated factors give lower benefits to spouses and former spouses who elect an early-starting FERS survivor annuity. It attributed the cut to the Board of Actuaries' revised mortality assumptions and treated none of 7 comments as significant adverse.

*[Federal Employees' Retirement System; Present Value Conversion Factors for Spouses of Deceased Separated Employees](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/20/2026-23729/federal-employees-retirement-system-present-value-conversion-factors-for-spouses-of-deceased) (Rule, 2026-11-20)*

- **OPM acknowledges the new factors cut monthly benefits for survivors who choose earlier annuities**. OPM agreed with one commenter that, compared to the prior factors, the updated factors yield lower benefit amounts for spouses or former spouses who elect an early-starting survivor annuity. The summary describes the table revision but never says benefits go down. (p. 75171)
- **OPM says the reductions stem from the Board of Actuaries' revised mortality assumptions and that federal law leaves it no discretion to keep the old factors**. OPM stated that the Board kept its 4.00 percent interest assumption but changed demographic assumptions, including mortality improvement, at its 2025 meeting. OPM said 5 U.S.C. 8461(i) requires it to update the factors when assumptions change, and rejected a commenter's claim that the rule was inherently controversial. (p. 75171)
- **OPM received 7 comments and treated none as significant adverse, rejecting objections from 3 commenters**. Two commenters supported the rule, two were unrelated, and three raised concerns about the factors, the missing examples, or the removed column. OPM concluded none required reconsideration, new analysis, or a substantive change, so the rule took effect as scheduled. (p. 75170)

## 3. OPM keeps eFile mandate for FLSA claims, rejecting a commenter's automatic-tolling proposal

OPM confirmed that the requirement to file FLSA claims through eFile took effect September 29, 2026. It declined to add automatic tolling while exemption requests are pending, offering case-by-case relief instead, and said exemption requests can be made by phone or email.

*[FLSA Claims and Compliance](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/20/2026-23730/flsa-claims-and-compliance) (Rule, 2026-11-20)*

- **OPM declined to add automatic tolling or a response deadline for eFile exemption requests, leaving claimants dependent on OPM's discretion**. OPM rejected a commenter's proposed text that would automatically toll deadlines while an exemption request is pending. It said that would change how the back-pay claim date is set, and it offered case-by-case relief (reconsideration of cancelled claims or waiver of the 45-day period) instead. A claimant awaiting an exemption therefore still risks a lost back-pay date, though OPM says a timely good-faith contact will weigh in their favor. (p. 75167)
- **OPM now says eFile exemption requests can be made by phone or email, even though the rule text points only to OPM's website**. The codified text directs exemption requests "as instructed on OPM's website," and two commenters noted no non-electronic method appears there. OPM said a request can be made using the phone number or email address in the DFR's contact caption, and that it will treat a timely phone or written request the same as a website request. (p. 75168)
- **OPM confirmed the effective date is September 29, 2026, and called a contradictory "45 days" statement in the original preamble a drafting error**. The July 31 preamble said the rule would take effect 45 days after the comment period expired, which conflicted with the DATES caption. OPM said the "45 days" reference was a mistake and that September 29, 2026 was the operative date, and it did not treat the point as a significant adverse comment. (p. 75168)
