## 1. RIF appeals move to OPM; bump-and-retreat rights eliminated

For RIF notices issued on or after September 2, 2026, separated, demoted or furloughed employees appeal to OPM within 30 days instead of to MSPB. Assignment rights shrink to a single standard: a position held by a lower-standing employee in the same tenure group, within 3 grades (5 for 30%+ disabled veterans). Union grievance routes in agreements executed on or after that date are treated as unenforceable.

*[Implementing the Revised Reduction in Force Regulations: Updated Workforce Reshaping Handbook](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/implementing-the-revised-reduction-in-force-regulations-updated-workforce-reshaping-handbook.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-16)*

- **RIF appeals for post-Sept. 2 notices go to OPM, not MSPB**. Employees separated, demoted, or furloughed under RIF notices issued on or after September 2, 2026 appeal to OPM (30-day window, electronic filing). Notices issued earlier stay with the Merit Systems Protection Board. The memo's summary does not mention the change in appeal forum. (p. 111)
- **Bump-and-retreat eliminated; one assignment-right standard (3 grades, 5 for 30%+ disabled veterans)**. A released competitive service employee may be assigned only to a position held by a lower-standing employee in the same tenure group, within 3 grades below (5 for preference eligibles with a 30%+ service-connected disability), if qualified. Categorical subgroup displacement chains are gone. (p. 185)
- **Union grievance procedures for RIFs unenforceable in agreements executed on or after Sept. 2, 2026**. Negotiated grievance routes for RIFs survive only for agreements operative before September 2, 2026 (and RIFs noticed before that date). Later agreements that provide such a process are to be treated as unenforceable because they conflict with the OPM appeal procedures. (p. 238)

## 2. OPM ends FEVS; agencies face new staffing reviews and CHCO certification

OPM stops administering FEVS; each executive agency runs its own 10-question survey, submits results to OPM and OMB within 90 days, and posts them publicly. Agency heads must certify in writing at each Annual Staffing Review that the CHCO has required oversight and control. The FAQ exempts agencies without a statutory CHCO from that certification, but the rule text contains no such exemption.

*[Final Regulations on Personnel Management in Agencies and Strategic Human Capital Management](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/final-regulations-on-personnel-management-in-agencies-and-strategic-human-capital-management.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-10) · [Guidance on Annual Employee Survey Data Submission Requirements and Deadlines Purpose](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/guidance-on-annual-employee-survey-data-submission-requirements-and-deadlines.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-10)*

- **FEVS discontinued; agencies run their own 10-question survey and post results publicly**. OPM stops administering the Federal Employee Viewpoint Survey. Each executive agency administers its own Annual Employee Survey with 10 prescribed questions, down from 16 (four unchanged, one reworded, five new). Agencies submit results to OPM and OMB within 90 days of completing administration and post them publicly. Agencies choose timing, platform and methodology, but the December 31 data-collection deadline is unchanged. (p. 12)
- **Agency heads must certify CHCO access and authority in writing at each ASR**. At each Annual Staffing Review the agency head certifies in writing to OPM that the CHCO has the required visibility, oversight and control of human capital functions. Any gap must be documented with a remediation timeline. The first certification is due at the first ASR after the rule takes effect, and OPM will announce that date. Agencies without a statutory CHCO are exempt from the certification. (p. 11)
- **CHCO memo conflicts with the rule on certification for agencies without a statutory chco.** CHCO memo: “annual certification requirement in § 250.206(b) does not apply to these agencies.” (p. 11) · Rule: “As part of each Annual Staffing Review, each agency must submit a written statement to OPM signed by the agency head certifying that” (p. 68025)

## 3. OPM can impose forced rating distributions on SL/ST senior professionals

The ban on forced distribution of SL/ST ratings is removed. OPM may set caps that agencies must apply, starting with the FY 2026 cycle. The memo describes agency waiver requests in extraordinary circumstances, while the rule preamble describes only a Presidential waiver. Mandatory higher-level review of Level 1 ratings becomes an optional, employee-requested review that cannot change the rating.

*[Issuance of Final Rule on Managing Senior Professional Performance](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/issuance-of-final-rule-on-managing-senior-professional-performance.pdf) (CHCO memo, 2026-11-09)*

- **OPM can impose mandatory forced distribution of SL/ST ratings, starting with the FY 2026 cycle**. The prior prohibition on forced or standardized distribution of senior professional ratings is removed. OPM may set distribution caps for some or all rating levels, which agencies must apply. The requirement applies to the FY 2026 appraisal closeout, only days after the Sept. 21, 2026 guidance and before the rule's Nov. 18 effective date. Agencies may request a waiver only in 'extraordinary circumstances'. Schedule C and G appointees are excluded. (p. 2)
- **CHCO memo conflicts with the rule on waiver of standardized distribution caps.** CHCO memo: “In such cases, the agency may submit a request for waiver of the distribution caps using the template included in the annual guidance memorandum.” (p. 2) · Rule: “provides that the President may waive the standardized distribution requirement by certifying that the performance of the agency's senior professionals was outstanding during the relevant time period.” (p. 65908)
- **Mandatory higher-level review of Level 1 (Unacceptable) ratings is replaced by an optional, employee-requested review that cannot change the rating**. Previously, any Level 1 rating required review and approval by a higher-level management official. Now review happens only if the senior professional requests it. The reviewer cannot change the rating and can only recommend a different one to the PRB. OPM calls this more robust, but a mandatory safeguard for Level 1 ratings is removed. (p. 2)
